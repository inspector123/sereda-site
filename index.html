How I work with coding agents

I use OMP, an open source agent harness. I forked Happy Coder, an open source mobile client for Claude Code, and rewrote the backend as an OMP extension, so the app on my phone talks to the same OMP session that's running on my computer. Messages from the phone go in through pi.sendUserMessage, and the app gets OMP's own events back instead of reading the terminal.

  • Tool calls, reasoning and subagents show up as they happen.
  • I can switch models and answer the agent's questions from the phone, including sudo password prompts.
  • A session started on the phone can be picked up later at my desk.

The recording is one prompt from the phone, running on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on my own GPUs at about 94 tok/s. It fixes two bugs I planted, messes up one of its own edits and fixes that too, then runs the tests. Not edited, 73 seconds, recorded on an Android emulator against a separate test server.

Models

I switch models depending on which one is best at the moment. I was on GPT-5.6 while it led the benchmarks, then Opus 5.5 pulled ahead and I went back to Claude. I use GPT for a second opinion and side tasks. Gemini does around 300 tokens a second, which is nice for quick stuff, but it doesn't go as deep.

Local models

A box at home with two 3090s and two CMP 170HX cards runs Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and GLM-5.3-Flash on vLLM. I set up the configs myself. Qwen does about 90 to 100 tok/s at FP8 and over 200 at 4 bit. I tracked a repeating-token bug in one setup down to vLLM itself. Given one thing at a time it's pretty close to Opus. It falls short on root cause analysis and edge cases.
